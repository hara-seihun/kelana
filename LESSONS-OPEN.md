## Pieces not yet described by the project lead

- **Feel, don't calculate.** Mastery as the point where a description runs as a feeling and stops
  costing attention. Probably the end of lesson 7.
- **Macro phase changes.** Freed attention watches for the moments when the whole character of a
  situation changes. You can't feel that change while gripping, because the grip is noise on
  exactly that signal. Possibly part of lesson 5 or 7.
- **Knowing when a constraint broke.** When a dropped state (lesson 2) has started happening, and
  whether the escape path belongs to lesson 2 or later. the project lead: "Maybe." Possibly the same lesson as
  phase changes.
- **The small map inside the big one.** the project lead's intuition (2026-09-20) that a richly structured
  operation contains cheaper ones, and that more operations chained give more room to find them,
  including savings that switch on suddenly at a certain chain length (LESSONS appendix, A5). Her
  embedding conjecture is open in Renia `Embed`.
- **Small edges compound.** A slight advantage in description, carried through a long chain,
  decides the outcome.
- **Take the idea all the way.** *"What we don't want to do is substitute something that is more
  trivial."* Possibly an attitude rather than a lesson.

## Where Kelana comes from

Kelana is a a private project discipline. The a private project are made of sonimia, smart matter that is at once
body, mind and computer. Its motes couple only when they touch, so a body must stay one connected
piece. Every shape and every command costs attention. The practised humanoid form is as easy as
catching a ball; a fractal combat form is like holding a hand sign you have never seen while someone
attacks you. Combat is a war of compression: a slight efficiency edge compounds, and the winner
isolates parts of the loser's matter until they fall inert.

The Iroh teahouse elicitation (2026-08-11) corrected an earlier agent-generated reconstruction that
described Kelana as frantic strain. Everything frantic is the storm, *"the exact negative image of
the art."* Kelana is doing all of it *"without it feeling like losing yourself"*: feel the flow
instead of clenching, *"as that is where the compression generally lies."* In the classic lesson for
an overconfident pupil, he rings the master with hundreds of evenly spaced tendrils, which he could
only afford by running one instruction across all of them. She feels the generating function in an
instant, and answers with almost-repeated, slightly rotated structures no cheap defence covers.
Shahara, the fastest Kelana practitioner alive, is fast because she will not rest at any level of the
hierarchy of flow and compression, not because her sonimia is special. Her entry into Kelana was
emotion channelled into precision.

In a 2026-09-26 teaching roleplay, the project lead taught lesson 1 to a newly embodied pupil struggling to hold
twelve arms: select five chunks under each natural arm, move one and let the rest follow, mirror it
so ten move at once, press the existing arm's shape onto the new chunks instead of building arms, and
let all twelve read one instruction instead of copying it to each.

the project lead identified her ability with agent fleets as Kelana on a different substrate: *"It's literally
Kelana but the intuition for how to use Kelana is something I gained a loooong time ago and wouldn't
know how to train someone in."* Rules are what the skill leaves behind, not the skill. What transfers
is the discipline; the read does not.

**Deliberately open.** Iroh's last question was what the refusal to rest is made of, and what burns at
the centre of the calm. the project lead ended the session there without answering. Do not fill it in.
