# One Q4 input coordinate for the complete routed layer-0 expert bank

The 16-expert shared diagonal looked useful locally. This experiment completes that question on **all 256 installed layer-0 gate/up experts**, every selected slot on 113 train and 126 disjoint held real-text producer tokens, and the *eight-output score-weighted sum*. One train-derived power-of-two diagonal is shared by every expert and by gate and up. Unlike the 16-expert panel, **no slot is left at the native output**. The all-bank construction improves the matched symmetric-Q4/Q4 recode, but does not approach the quality of the unchanged installed GGUF gate/up bank with one shared Q8 input code. No native reader or full-model quality experiment is warranted for this frozen Q4 recode.

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Installed decoded GGUF gate/up + shared group-32 Q8 input | .005653 | **.005431** |
| Installed decoded GGUF gate/up + shared group-32 Q4 input | .098869 | .093491 |

The selected diagonal cuts held Q4/Q4 error by 32.44%, yet its error is **13.65 times** that of GGUF/Q8. The other predeclared diagonal choices alpha .5/1/2 score .093854/.079843/.079970 on held. Train and held both select alpha 1.5; the held panel did not choose it. Only 109/2,048 input exponents are nonzero at that setting. The bank-wide weight maximum changes the coordinate from the 16-expert fit (159 changed channels there), but sharing is preserved across *every* chosen expert, not merely a frequent subset. The train split selects 904 slots covering 169 experts, held selects 1,008 covering 188 experts; the 35 held-only expert IDs receive the frozen common diagonal and their own weight recodes, not special cases.

## Map, image rate and online bill

Inputs, scores, selected IDs and baseline per-slot down outputs are the native Qwen3.6-35B-A3B GGUF layer-0 route capture. All 256 gate/up pairs and the down weights are decoded from the pinned installed image with its installed `libggml-base.so` Q4_K/Q5_K decoders. For each captured input `x`, the reference computes every routed expert's decoded GGUF gate/up → SiLU·up → unchanged Q5_K down, then combines its eight outputs with the captured scores in float64 for the local comparison. The candidate instead uses `Q4(x·d)` and `Q4(W/d)` for both gate and up; its down and router score are unchanged. The quantizer uses group-32 symmetric nearest-even signed Q4 integers, FP16-rounded max scales and an actual packed-nibble round trip. GGUF/Q8, GGUF/Q4, Q4-weight/Q8-input and no-diagonal Q4/Q4 are matched controls. Offline evaluation materializes FP32 decoded arrays for BLAS, so the table is a local response comparison under that arithmetic, **not** native FP32 bit identity.

`d_j = 2^clip(round(alpha·(log2(max_expert,row |W_rj| / max_train |x_j|) - median)/2),-3,3)` uses only train inputs and all installed gate/up coefficients. The max is scanned across every bank entry before either split is evaluated. The experiment's paid proposed gate/up image is 256 × (1,048,576 packed nibble + 131,072 FP16 group-scale) = **301,989,888 bytes**, the same physical byte count as the existing 256 Q4_K gate/up bank, plus one shared **4,096-byte FP16 diagonal**. Its hypothetical per-token Q4 input is **1,152 bytes**, versus **2,176 bytes** for the Q8 control, and would require 2,048 coordinate products (or priced power-of-two exponent changes) plus 64 dynamic group reductions per token. Each selected slot still needs 2,097,152 packed gate/up integer terms, a SiLU and 1,048,576 down terms; the eight weights and outputs are not skipped. These are precise format/work budgets, **not** a saved complete packed bank or measured bandwidth, decode latency or whole-model TPS. In particular, weight-stream bytes do not fall at all, and adding a distinct Q4 input preparation beside the selected Q8 path could increase online work. This was an early CPU quality gate, not a proposed engine image.

Per-expert slot counts and denominators are retained. The array shards preserve failed alternatives and let the eight-expert error be recomputed without repeated weight decoding.
