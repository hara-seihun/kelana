# Exact radix threshold for two nibble-head scores

The signed-byte one-dot proposal in [the paired-query study](../nibble-paired-query/README.md) fails for every radix that fits its operand. How much wider does an exact radix have to be? For 32 independently variable signed-nibble key and query coordinates in `[-7,7]`, the **smallest positive integer radix with no two-score fiber collision is 3,099**. The earlier 3,137 construction is a convenient balanced-remainder decoder, not the smallest injective encoding. Both need a signed-16-bit packed query; the distinction saves no native dot instruction.

## Domain and proof

For a fixed key `c`, the required integer observation is `(U,V)=(u·c,v·c)`. A proposed single scalar dot produces `D=(u+Bv)·c=U+BV`. The decoder is allowed to know `c`, but not to perform another query-dependent key dot. Two valid inputs with the same key collide exactly when their query differences `du,dv ∈ [-14,14]^32` satisfy `du·c=-B(dv·c)` with `dv·c ≠ 0`. Every such difference vector is realizable by two vectors in `[-7,7]^32`.

At `B=3099`, `|du·c| ≤ 32·14·7=3136`, so a collision requires `|dv·c|=1`. If every `|c_j|=7`, `dv·c` is divisible by seven. Otherwise the sum of absolute key magnitudes must be at least `ceil(3099/14)=222`. Up to signs and permutation, its only possibilities are 31 sevens and one six, 31 sevens and one five, or 30 sevens and two sixes. The corresponding bounded integer forms are `7A+6b`, `7A+5b`, and `7A+6b`, with `|A|≤434, |b|≤14` in the first two and `|A|≤420, |b|≤28` in the last. None represents 3099: modulo seven the six cases require `b≡2`, whose largest admissible positive `b` is 9 or 23; the five case requires `b≡1`, whose largest is 8. Their maximum attainable values in these residues are 3092, 3078 and 3078 respectively. A smaller absolute-key sum has `|du·c|≤3094`. Thus distinct `(U,V)` cannot collide at 3099 for any key.

Every integer radix from 1 through 3098 does collide. For 1 through 448, take all key codes equal to one; choose `du·c=-B`, `dv·c=1`. For 449 through 3098, take 31 keys equal to seven and one equal to six, except at 3093 where the exceptional key is five. With one six, the attainable `|du·c|` set `7A+6b` for `|A|≤434, |b|≤14` contains every integer 449 through 3098 except 3093. With one five, `3093=7·434+5·11`. In the six case set `dv` to `+1` on a seven and `-1` on the six; in the five case set it to `-2` on a seven and `+3` on the five. Both produce `dv·c=1`. Split `A` among the 31 seven-key coordinates, at most 14 per coordinate, and set the exceptional difference to `b`. Negating `du` makes `D=0` while the two-score outputs differ. `bound.py` constructs all 3,098 witnesses, checks their differences and dot sums, and independently enumerates the four threshold key forms; [receipt.json](receipt.json) records the boundary.

This is a **full signed-nibble cube and positive integer radix** theorem, not a bound on actual model-reachable keys, approximate attention, nonlinear encodings, multi-instruction programs or a decoder's cost. Each head's real dynamic score scale is a separate operation. Integer scores do not certify FP32-bit-identical softmax.

## The consumer bill

At the minimum radix a coordinate `u_j+3099v_j` can reach `7·3100=21,700`, beyond signed byte and inside signed 16 bits; `|D|≤1568·3100=4,860,800` fits signed 32 bits. For the simpler `B=3137`, the balanced remainder in `[-1568,1568]` recovers `U`, then `(D-U)/B` recovers `V`. At `B=3099`, a plain balanced remainder can pick the wrong score near an endpoint: injectivity only promises a **key-dependent** decoder, not a cheap remainder instruction. The sixteen-bit query has to be split to use gfx1151's byte/nibble packed dot instructions. Even two signed-byte limbs against byte-expanded keys cost sixteen `v_dot4` instructions per 32-coordinate pair before combine and decode, versus eight `v_dot8` for two independent signed-nibble scores. Key expansion, dynamic query preparation and separate floating scales make the radix path still less attractive. No native timing is implied by that instruction count.

The theorem directs the next search away from exact integer-radix packing. If learned query and key codes share a *restricted reachable* set with a collision-free signed-byte label, certify its actual fibers on fresh quantized-producer keys and queries, then price a native decoder. The [quantized-upstream score transfer](../shared-query-producer-transfer/README.md) keeps the shared three-dot route as a stronger current quality/cost candidate than exact radix packing.
