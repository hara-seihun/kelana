# Sub-bit programme results

We have complete packed Qwen3-0.6B images at **.6245, .7600 and .8955 bits per unique parameter**, with every body matrix, the shared embedding/head and norms paid. Their language quality is still poor. We also have a directly consumed sub-bit projection that is faster than its binary-factor control, and a new shared V/O representation that removes most value-cache coordinates rather than reconstructing them.

These are different results. The programme has not produced a good sub-bit language model, a complete-model native speedup, or a win over a fully matched state-of-the-art quantization pipeline. The useful progress is a clearer representation/consumer design, stronger comparisons, and concrete failures that change what we should optimize next.

## Shared three-dot key scores

[The two-head shared query score](shared-query-base/README.md) replaces one of four signed-nibble key-score dot passes with a difference query: a signed-eight-bit base head takes two dots, and the other head adds one nibble dot of its prepared-query difference. Train-selected base heads give held layer-0/14 original-producer causal KL .278608/.334929, against .281052/.346220 for independent three-dot heads and .275431/.332396 for four dots on the same frozen paid keys. This saves 256 logical nibble products per cached key/layer without changing the key or weight image. It pays 256 query subtractions/token/layer, extra score additions and longer base-score lifetime. Native cost and quantized-producer model loss are unmeasured; a paired native panel is earned only after the score quality transfers.

## One-digit value-mass limit on the frozen image

[The direct 15-unit nibble consumer](value-nibble-one-digit/README.md) uses only one signed/unsigned packed-nibble dot pass against the existing rank-28 V cache. ## Exact sparse high-digit value mass

[The direct integer narrow-value consumer](value-integer-consumer/README.md) uses two dense signed-byte dots for its 4,095-count probabilities. [Sparse high-digit mass](value-mass-residual/README.md) splits each count into a dense low digit modulo 32 and a high digit on at most 127 causal keys per query/head. It preserves the integer attention vector exactly, without expanding nibble values. On four inspected validation captures, only 11.06%/8.67% of layer-0/14 causal key/head pairs have a nonzero high digit. An ideal compacted correction uses 44.47%/45.67% fewer signed-byte products than two dense dots. No native latency or composed-model quality follows: count prep, compaction, irregular cache reads and paid V/O production remain. The rarely eligible exact one-dot query switch is not the route. [Fixed whole-GQA-group wave assignment](value-low-occupancy-bound/README.md) additionally cuts four-lane held sparse-low/high issued slots by 13.0%/4.7% at layers 0/14 without increasing the number of distinct KV groups per wave. This is a globally optimal train grouping inside the fixed whole-group assignment grammar, not native time. Price a fused sparse correction with this assignment at occupied contexts, and fit the value image against the cheap consumer on quantized-producer text before shipping it.

## Next publication milestone

The next milestone is a useful complete-model quality/rate point with an executable native consumer, rather than another complete image that merely fits under one bit.

1. Repair the damaged quantized producer before native narrow-cache work. The [prefix ablation](full-model/PREFIX-ABLATION.md) puts most of the actionable loss at layer 0, especially its coupled V/O and MLP behavior. Fit a paid layer-0 composed image against gold or teacher behavior, then revisit narrow V/O on a viable producer. Exact restoration would cost another 0.40830 complete-model BPW and is only a diagnostic. Preserve the shared value coordinates through attention rather than rebuilding the original hidden dimension by habit.
2. Fit actual downstream behavior and rate allocation across module families. The all-matrix residual experiment rejects local response improvement as a sufficient selection rule. Compare retained original-producer fits with quantized-producer training under the same calibration budget.
3. Strengthen the full-model competitor's reconstruction training and add an activation-aware scalar/structured control. Keep NanoQuant adaptation, full NanoQuant, cheap scalar LS and QAT claims separate.
4. Retain paid rate, calibration work and native boundary costs together. A lower rank with wider coefficients can beat one-bit factors on instructions even when its nominal precision is higher. Conversely, extra factor stages can spend an attractive byte saving in launches and scratch.
5. Broaden text and model sizes after a construction retains useful language behavior. Tau-style task scaling needs a meaningful unquantized task baseline and non-collapsed quantized models; the current .625-bit model is below that threshold.

The [Bonsai/Kelana programme](README.md) and its existing continuing lane own the next iterations. No Bonsai serving or numerical default changed in these studies.
