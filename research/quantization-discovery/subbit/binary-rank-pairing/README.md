# Rank-group reconstruction is the wrong objective for the paid binary map

A zero-rate permutation of the 384 intermediate coordinates changes which rank responses share a 32-coordinate A7 quantizer. The [energy-sorted rank gauge](../binary-rank-gauge/README.md) already improves the frozen `mlp_up` response. I tested whether fitting those groups to the intermediate A7 reconstruction, rather than merely sorting training response energy, improves the complete two-factor map. It does on training inputs and loses on held inputs at both tested layers. This is a measured objective mismatch, not a rejection of rank regrouping.

## Construction and cost

For a frozen paid image `diag(post) U V diag(pre)`, a rank permutation `P` changes its stored signs to `PV` and `UP⁻¹`. The unquantized real map is identical. The first factor produces its integer rank outputs directly in the new order. The second factor consumes that order without an online gather. The one-bit factor payload, scales, two A7 quantizers, signed bitplane dots, rank intermediate and group count are unchanged. Packing and unpacking both permuted sign planes is checked for each arm; the receipt identifies the resulting packed bytes. The changed group maxima and signed-int8 rounding deliberately change the approximate map. No native scheduling or model quality is claimed.

The first stage and its train integer responses are fixed. Starting from the training-energy sort, 6,000 seeded proposals swap two ranks in different groups. I accept a swap only if it lowers the **sum of squared real-coordinate reconstruction errors** over the 64 training rank responses. Each proposal recomputes just its two affected groups. The global maximum is invariant under a permutation; for each group, the existing two-choice A7 ladder, clipping and integer multiplier reconstruct the rank values at exactly the stage-two common scale. This is a greedy local search, not a global optimum. It does not read held inputs, `U`, output scales or teacher outputs to choose a swap. Those exclusions are precisely why the objective can fail downstream. If `e₁` is the first-stage error and `e₂` the second-stage reconstruction error in the original rank order, final squared error is `(e₁+e₂)ᵀG(e₁+e₂)` with `G=Uᵀdiag(post²)U`, summed over inputs and with the corresponding first-stage common scale. The search minimizes only `e₂ᵀe₂`. Its missing off-diagonal terms and its missing cross term with `e₁` can reverse the ranking even on training data; here training final error happens to improve but held error reverses.

The experiment supplies two controls against a misleading result. The original energy sort's held RMS reproduces the earlier gauge report on the same windows, and the separately fitted threshold pair keeps its previous gain in both layouts. Yet optimizing the intermediate error reverses some of the energy sort's held advantage. The output factor's correlated signs and first-stage error are part of the complete map; intermediate MSE ignores both. Even complete-response training selection can overfit these small captures, as the [group selector study](../binary-group-choice/README.md) independently found.
