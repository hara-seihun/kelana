# A ceiling on selecting frozen complete-model gains

The .624513-BPW Qwen3-0.6B image has three already measured first-layer down/O-scale arms on the *same* complete-model windows: unchanged 1/1, prefix-selected 2/1.75, and complete-train-selected 2/1.5. How much could a perfect window-level switch among them rescue? The answer is small even if the switch cheats and sees every gold token before choosing. Change the paid signs, right basis and observing MLP rather than build a selector for these three images.

For each fixed 256-token window, let `L_i(a)` be its mean next-token gold NLL under arm `a` (255 predictions). A selector chooses one arm before forwarding the entire window. Its best achievable empirical loss, *even with the gold answers*, is `mean_i min_a L_i(a)`. This equality is an exact finite-family certificate: the objective separates by window, and choosing each minimizer attains it. A realizable selector without gold cannot beat this number on these windows. It is **not** a bound for token-level switching, other gain values or newly learned codes. Changing an arm's gains does not change the 46,530,084-byte stored payload or its native factor-term count; a real conditional selector would additionally pay for its decision and state transitions.

| Frozen matched split, eight 256-token windows | Base 1/1 | Complete-train 2/1.5 | Prefix-selected 2/1.75 | Gold-label window oracle | Max oracle gain over base |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Validation 8–15 | 9.079709 | 8.950998 | **8.881914** | 8.807895 | 0.271814 |
| Test 16–23 | 9.416896 | 9.368507 | **9.292967** | 9.150627 | 0.266269 |

On test the oracle adds at most .142340 nat over the best frozen constant gain, and .266269 over the unchanged base. It picks 2/1.5 on three windows, 2/1.75 on two, and the *unchanged* 1/1 on three. Window 20 alone accounts for .638858 nat of the oracle's difference from constant 2/1.75, so the constant-arm average conceals material heterogeneity. On validation the additional ceiling over the best constant is .074019 nat. These are optimistic, in-sample ceilings for the inspected windows, not an achievable generalizing gain and not a universal bound over continuous scale choices.

The train receipts contain nine predeclared pairs on eight different train windows. Leave-one-train-window-out mean selection picks 2/1.5 on five folds and 2/2 on three. The original all-eight mean selects 2/1.5. That instability and the exact held oracle ceiling together make another common-gain classifier a poor next use of compute. The stronger experiment is a train-gold or teacher-driven **coupled code fit** of the already-paid layer-0 narrow V/O and MLP gate/up/down images with the later binary body and tied endpoints in the loop, followed by fresh whole-model loss. The oracle does not say such changed images are limited to .266 nat.

`oracle.py` recomputes the finite optimum using only Python's standard library and checks consecutive window indices, unique token hashes, arm lists, source/input hashes, unchanged payload and zero term delta.
