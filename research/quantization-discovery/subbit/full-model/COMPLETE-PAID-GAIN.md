# Complete-model selection of paid first-layer gains

The first-layer down/O gains selected with the original downstream did not transfer reliably to the complete .624513-BPW image. I selected those same two existing scale coordinates with **all 28 binary body layers, quantized norms and the shared quantized embedding/head in the forward pass**. This repairs the selection boundary but not the model. The complete-train optimum is down gain 2, narrow-O gain 1.5; on eight later test windows it improves base NLL by only .04839 and loses by .07554 to the earlier prefix-selected down 2/O 1.75. Validation agrees with that ranking. Another scalar gain sweep is not the next useful iteration.

The nine predeclared pairs use down gains {1, 1.5, 2} and O gains {1, 1.5, 2}. Eight pinned WikiText train windows, 255 next-token labels each, choose the minimum mean gold NLL. The original packed two-bit factor codes, right factors and the count of FP16 output scale slots stay fixed. Gains multiply the existing down output-row scales and narrow O left-row scales; BF16 expansion is used for the quality forward. The quantized tied head correction is applied identically after every arm. Complete payload is **46,530,084 bytes, .62451258 bits per unique parameter**, with zero change in native factor terms, logical value width or scale loads. The earlier 1.75 arm is an outside-grid, previously selected comparator, not a train candidate. Rounding each scaled vector back to FP16 is part of the map.

The selected arm wins against base on four of eight test and six of eight validation windows. The prefix arm wins on five and six. Across the eight test windows, selected-minus-base is −.04839 NLL with paired standard error .18133; selected-minus-prefix is +.07554 with standard error .06718. The selected 2/1.5 arm wins only three of eight test windows directly against 2/1.75. On validation selected-minus-base is −.12871 and selected-minus-prefix +.06908. These are small, heterogeneous samples, not evidence that 1.75 is universally optimal. They do show that selecting a common scale gain on eight actual complete-model train windows fails to pick the better of these two paid gains on both held splits. Test 16–23 and validation 8–15 were not used to choose or update the gain.

This negative is restricted to a **two-common-gain, frozen-code, nine-point training grammar** and its BF16-expanded gold-loss observation. It says nothing about paid per-head scales, changed gate/up/down signs, learned right factors or native execution. The complete model remains badly damaged regardless of this gain choice. The next experiment should train the existing layer-0 code/scale slots against composed loss with both downstream body and tied image quantized, using more independent train windows and a separate selection split. If that retains a substantial held quality gain, compare its narrow-coordinate consumer with int4 expansion. Fitting more common gains on these same eight train windows is unlikely to repair the interaction.
