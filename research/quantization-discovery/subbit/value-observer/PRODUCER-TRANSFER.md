# The layer-14 narrow value image meets a different producer

The frozen 192-coordinate V/O images were trained on original Qwen3-0.6B layer inputs. I replaced all body projections and layer norms in layers 0 through 13 with the existing .62454-BPW refined binary image, while leaving the tied embedding and every part of layer 14 original. On the first four train and four validation WikiText windows, I captured the BF16 input to layer 14 Q. This is the *same* input to its K and V projections. Its cosine against the corresponding original-producer capture is 0.434 on both splits; its squared norm is 1.41 and 1.47 times the original. The producer distribution has moved far enough that the old image's local result is no longer a safe guide.

With layer-14 original Q/K, normalization, RoPE and original V/O providing the causal post-O target **on those new inputs**, the frozen selected 192-coordinate image scores .7651 train / .9202 validation relative squared error. The equal-byte, equally refitted uniform-rank-24 image scores .7952 / .9012. The old, more expensive 224-coordinate image scores .7651 / .8948. The selected image's original-producer held advantage over uniform (.3304 against .3404 on the prior four-window validation) reverses on this quantized-producer validation. All coefficients and bitpacked images were frozen for this comparison.

I then refitted each 192-coordinate image's paid two-bit output codes and FP16 row/head scales for two sweeps on **only the four quantized-producer train windows**. The right codes, right scales, rank, packed bytes and attention probabilities remain fixed. Both arms still store 183,552 parameter bytes, or .466797 BPW over V/O, and compute 589,824 signed-grid terms and 384 logical BF16 value-cache bytes per token. The selected image improves from .7651 to .0174 train and .9202 to .4844 validation; uniform improves from .7952 to .0186 train and .9012 to .4926 validation. Selected now wins the held aggregate by .00819 and three of four held windows, but the .017 training error and one .951 held window make the calibration gap obvious. This is a real matched-rate observer improvement, not a language-loss or latency result. The train/held gap is larger than the difference between allocations.

The comparison's teacher changes with the input: it is the original layer-14 causal output evaluated on that same quantized upstream activation. It is *not* the original model's desired layer-14 output, so this fit cannot by itself repair upstream damage or claim full-model NLL. The quantized producers are complete upstream body/norms, not a Gaussian perturbation. The model's head and embeddings stay original, and no layer-14 projection changes except in the CPU observer. The causal replay uses FP32 matrix products with BF16 rounding of projected narrow V, whereas the capture itself is BF16 HF SDPA. There is no native compressed consumer in this experiment.

`producer_transfer.py capture` writes the BF16 input bits and a source/model/image/token receipt. `producer_transfer.py score` reopens both paid input images, fits and repacks the new images, checks the decoded output coefficients, and writes every held window score and hashes to `/path/to/workspace/data/kelana-subbit/value-observer/layer14-quantized-upstream-transfer.json`. Its input capture is `layer14-quantized-upstream.npz`; the two new packed images are `layer14-quantized-upstream-{selected192,uniform192}.npz`. Use Bonsai's GPU reservation for capture, and a CPU invocation for score:

```sh
P=/path/to/workspace/data/fish-s2-pro/venv/bin/python
D=research/quantization-discovery/subbit/value-observer/producer_transfer.py
/path/to/workspace/projects/bonsai-halo/tools/run-batch-compare --runtime-max 47s --exec "$P" "$D" capture
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 "$P" "$D" score
```
