## What we are optimizing

A candidate consists of stored model-specific codes and a program that consumes them with an activation. The program may use learned dictionaries, binary factors, a lattice, a trellis, shared labels or a composition across several model operations. Individual floating weights and int4 intermediates need not appear online.

Search the Pareto frontier of held-out model loss, actual stored bits, online cost and quantization/training cost. Equivalently, minimize model loss subject to a storage budget, a latency budget and a calibration-compute budget. A row reconstruction norm is a useful surrogate, not the objective that decides which quantizer wins.

The hardware program belongs inside the search. Codewords that have equal representational capacity can have very different lookup, instruction, register and boundary costs. Small exact assignment problems can supply optimal solutions within a frozen family. Designing the family, allocating its rate and finding useful approximate model behavior are the larger questions. We do not need a general compiler or a universal optimality proof before trying a construction.

Initial families worth competing:

- Response codebooks shared by many output rows. Compute their responses for the current activation, then consume short labels directly. Learn both the codebook and an inexpensive way to produce its response table.
- Binary-factor compositions, with rank, scale placement and intermediate encoding selected together. Sub-bit storage can arise from the complete factorization rather than a fractional scalar alphabet.
- Algebraic and finite-state codebooks with many effective weight vectors but cheap generated responses. Search compatible consumers as well as local decoders.
- Mixed-rate or residual constructions where the quality recovered by an exception pays for its index, payload and irregular execution. Rate allocation should reflect downstream sensitivity.

A failed calibration fit should lead to a better model of the activation distribution or a more expressive family, not a requirement to preserve every possible integer input exactly. A native kernel losing to int4 should change the representation/program design, not end the search for direct inference.

Fit scaling relationships only once several rates, sizes and independent samples exist; retain the points and residuals rather than announcing a law from two endpoints.

Start with teacher-forced loss and perplexity, then a fixed set of short reasoning and tool-use tasks. Tau2 or its current successor is useful once the unquantized model has meaningful baseline success. At a near-zero task floor, it cannot tell two damaged quantizers apart. Pin simulator, task version and decoding settings before running an agent benchmark.

## What counts as beating a comparator

The [frontier study](frontier/README.md) owns the primary-source receipts and implementation shortlist. NanoQuant is the first sub-bit PTQ adversary. LittleBit and ParetoQ provide separate reconstruction-training/QAT targets. QTIP and QuIP# supply strong structured low-bit controls; T-MAC is a direct-lookup CPU execution competitor. A scalar round-to-nearest int4 control alone is too weak to support a superiority claim.

Compare the same source model, tokenizer, data, quantization scope and compute budget. A model-specific quantizer may inspect all its known weights. Hold out activation/text/task examples, not arbitrary weight rows, unless dictionary transfer to unseen matrices is the actual question. Report PTQ and retraining separately. A changed upstream method is a named adaptation until its relevant procedure and budget match. Literature numbers on a different model or GPU are context, not wins.

Report both matrix payload rate and complete model rate. Complete storage includes dictionaries, scales, rotations, factors, masks, exceptions, padding, embeddings, norms and output head. Qwen's tied embedding/head is a large fraction of this small model: keeping it at BF16 can dominate the entire bit budget. Also report runtime scratch and KV separately. An expanded BF16 reference is useful for quality evaluation, but its timing says nothing about compressed inference.

For a promising family, measure a direct native consumer including activation-dependent preparation, all factor passes, intermediate writes, decoding or relabeling at real consumer boundaries, and output reduction. Compare against the same quantized map expanded to a conventional arithmetic path, and against a strong quality-matched int4 implementation. CPU and gfx1151 receipts stay separate. Prefill and decode have different reuse and may choose different representations.

## Results and active questions

[The exact two-head score-radix bound](paired-score-radix/README.md) proves that the smallest collision-free positive integer radix for a single paired dot over the full 32-coordinate signed-nibble cube is 3,099. It checks collisions at every smaller radix, including all seventeen that fit signed-byte query operands. The minimum still needs signed-16-bit query lanes, and its key-dependent decoder is not the earlier cheap 3,137 balanced remainder. This closes the exact radix grammar, not approximate learned joint scores or a whole-region ISA bound.

[Complete post-O base-head selection](shared-score-post-o-selection/README.md) exhausts all 256 frozen group-base choices for that quantized-upstream three-dot consumer. Train response selection worsens held post-O error .02058570→.02059130 against train causal-KL selection, and even a held-only oracle reaches only .02057292 versus four-dot .02048744. This is a finite-family stop rule for frozen base choice, not a rejection of jointly learned Q/K and score labels.

[The shared three-dot Q/K consumer on a quantized upstream producer](shared-query-producer-transfer/README.md) retains most four-dot causal and post-O quality at Qwen3-0.6B layer 14, without changing paid Q/K or the 128-byte signed-nibble key cache. Its held causal KL is .278158 against .276883 for four dots at 25% fewer key-score products. The query subtraction and score additions remain unpaid natively; the damaged prefix also makes fresh full-model loss and jointly fitted Q/K the next quality decision.

- [Response codebooks](response-codebooks/README.md) explore learned and algebraically generated short-vector labels at .378 to .9 total BPW. The direct consumer is retained, but this family loses to the matched binary factors at lower rate on the three fair Qwen comparisons. A scalar CPU table lowering also loses to VNNI. More kernel work on that version is not the next step.
- [Shared Q/K/V factors](binary-factors/shared-qkv.md) improve K/V response but worsen Q at an exactly matched .44922 aggregate BPW. Halving first-stage work does not halve total work: the wider second factor spends the saving. Sharing alone does not establish a faster quantizer.
- [Learned butterflies](fast-transform/README.md) have cheap input transforms and conditionally optimal binary row coefficients, but lose the .55-bit response comparison. [Pattern-coded input factors](block-factor/README.md) recover much of the capacity, especially when only quiet groups are compressed. They still lose to equally activation-fitted binary factors. No kernel is planned for that version.
- [Spectral plus binary residuals](spectral-residual/README.md) reserve a few real-valued response modes within the same byte budget. Giving both competitors the same zero-byte output-scale fit leaves hybrid gains on 8 of 16 .55-bit matrices, rather than a universal improvement. The scale fit itself improves all sixteen binary controls.
- [Post-O head-rate allocation](post-o-allocation/README.md) solves a sixteen-head quadratic over frozen two/three-bit Q responses at .40778 BPW. Train-only selection lowers held post-O squared error from .024280 for the attention-KL mask to .022787 without extra bytes or factor terms. It needs fresh captures and complete-model validation before a native mixed-bit consumer.
- [Attention-metric fitting](attention-metric/README.md) changes existing four-bit codes for normalized Q and actual attention consumers. It improves downstream observations despite worse raw projection error. The completed model continuation and native panel are in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md).
- [Quantized response subspaces and calibration curves](SPECTRAL.md) show a rank/precision/work trade and how fixed representations improve from 256 to 2,048 training vectors. Further model sizes and calibration data are needed for scaling laws.
- [The tied head and embedding](tied-head/README.md) has a shared .8953-bit codebook/exact-row image that beats plain scalar RTN2 on fixed head/embedding observations. [Mixed precision](tied-factors/README.md) loses head-only NLL but wins when the same image also supplies quantized embeddings through the model. [A code-label head bias](tied-code-bias/README.md) cannot replace the frequency-bucket correction by reusing a frozen segment label: train-calibration selection prefers the global offset, and the best label arm loses held NLL and KL. Selection must see both consumers.
- [Complete-model images](full-model/README.md), the [batched fitter](batched-fit/README.md), [scale repair](model-tuning/README.md) and [full scalar controls](full-scalar/README.md) establish the actual complete rate and quality boundary. The .62454-bit scale-repaired image still has test NLL 9.117 versus 3.311 original. [Rank-8/16 residuals](model-residual/README.md) spend more bits and improve every local response metric without improving complete test loss.
- [Narrow shared values](value-observer/README.md) use one rank-28 basis for each pair of GQA output consumers at .53060 V/O BPW. They improve both tested layers' causal-output distortion and sharply improve layer-0 model continuation over independent binary V/O. A direct consumer can keep the smaller value coordinate throughout attention; native latency and broad model transfer remain open.

The next iteration should take a concrete part of [the next milestone](RESULTS.md#next-publication-milestone): quantized-producer composed-map fitting, complete rate allocation, a stronger reconstructed comparator, or a priced narrow-value consumer. All-layer capture, the first complete images and larger fresh Q evaluation are done. Reuse them rather than repeat the first commissioning round.

The [peer board](../../../../bonsai-halo/orchestration/README.md) owns live claims; these references explain the research questions. New lane researchers should take a distinct construction, allocation problem or comparator with a concrete deliverable. Prefer sub-bit research over another unrelated engine tuning iteration.
