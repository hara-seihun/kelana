# The seven-exp router on actual Qwen routes

The real-arithmetic shortcut in [the router observer](README.md) had only captured selected scores, not the 256 logits or the producer vectors. This replay joins the installed GGUF's F32 layer-0 router rows with 113 train and 126 held normalized producer vectors. It compares raw-logit top-eight against captured native IDs, then computes seven exponentials and an eight-term sum in NumPy FP32. The score comparison uses captured normalized native FP32 scores. Its output comparison uses all eight captured unweighted down vectors, so it observes the entire weighted routed sum rather than just scores.

| Split | Raw routes matching capture | Routes separated by FP32 dot envelope | Maximum score-coordinate difference | Mean / maximum weighted-sum relative L2 change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Train, 113 tokens | 113 | 112 | 1.13249e-6 | 1.29815e-6 / 2.97427e-6 |
| Held, 126 tokens | 126 | 121 | 1.37091e-6 | 1.40910e-6 / 5.59192e-6 |

The smallest eighth-to-ninth *real-logit midpoint* margin is .001467 on train and .000835 on held. For a dot of 2,048 F32 products, the script uses `gamma_2048 = 2048*2^-24/(1-2048*2^-24)` times the sum of absolute products, plus a doubled FP64 evaluation envelope. On 233 of 239 rows, the least selected lower endpoint exceeds every unselected upper endpoint. Thus every dot evaluation respecting that absolute-error model selects the same top-eight *raw logits* there. Six rows fail the sufficient test; their captured IDs also agree with raw selection. This certificate does not include rounding of a 256-way softmax or establish the native top-k instruction order. Normal finite FP32 inputs/products and no overflow are assumptions of its dot bound. The output differences are measurements on this capture, not bounds on future inputs or downstream loss.

The score arm starts from FP64 dot midpoints cast to FP32; it subtracts the selected maximum, calls NumPy FP32 `exp`, sums eight terms and divides in FP32. It is an **alternative numerical map**, not the installed fused CUDA kernel. In particular a raw-logit near tie can disagree with rounded-softmax top-k, as the existing synthetic witness shows. The current fused router is faster than the unfused width-invariant control on the recorded depth-1024 decode panel; replacing it with another unfused path would miss the opportunity. A native fused raw-top-eight arm is justified for a bounded full-model quality/time comparison, but not for selection now. Compare all forty layers, prompt and batched paths, and the MTP near-tie prefix before considering a serving default.
