# Shared output coordinates for Qwen's routed experts

[The post-MoE RMSNorm fiber theorem](rms-observer/README.md) proves that the installed positive-epsilon, nonzero-gain *complete-vector* ideal-real observer is injective: an exact positive-ray quotient cannot discard the routed sum's radial information when residual/shared state is fixed. Its explicit inverse is a semantic witness, not a free native decoder or a statement about FP32 bits or final logits.

[The complete forty-layer actual-route packed-code census](code-sharing-all-route/README.md) finds no repeated full Q4_K gate/up block within 5,120 observed eight-expert layer/token routes in either split. Even granting free same-position reuse of matching four-byte low-code fragments with mismatched metadata, held savings are at most 114,356 code bytes/token or 0.00435446% of conditional complete one-read weights; layer 0 supplies 99.85% of matches. Frozen byte sharing is not the missing native MoE speedup; changed trained labels and direct packed consumption remain different questions.

[The exact score-observation rank witness](score-observation-rank/README.md) shows that all 5,120 actual forty-layer routed weighted sums preserve seven independent normalized-score directions: nonzero modular minors of the captured FP32 output differences certify that a smooth score-only carrier with fewer than seven real coordinates cannot recover the complete sum on a fixed-output simplex neighborhood. This lower bound is semantic, not seven native exponentials or a speedup.

[Normalized router observation](router-observer/README.md) factors the real-arithmetic 256-way softmax, top eight and selected-weight normalization through eight IDs and seven logit differences. The clamp is provably inactive: selected mass is at least 1/32. It reduces exponential evaluations from 256 to seven per row, but changes FP32 rounding and has no measured native speed or complete-model quality. The selected runtime remains unchanged.

[The actual-route Jacobian certificate](routed-jacobian/README.md) now includes the installed F32 router. At one held layer-0 producer input, the complete normalized eight-expert weighted sum has full local rank 2,048, certified by an inverse-residual bound of .781665 below one. The exact differentiable shared input bottleneck therefore needs all 2,048 real coordinates on this fixed-route neighborhood. The older router-independent bound was 2,041. Neither is a native FP32, packed-bit or throughput bound.

[Paired routed gate/up rank](paired-gate-rank/README.md) certifies the co-routed pair's exact real rank is 2,043 of 2,048 input coordinates, with five checked BF16 signed-row identities matching the modular lower bound. [An actual held route with three original BF16 experts](route-input-rank/README.md) fills all **2,048** input dimensions by a nonzero finite-field minor, closing the pair's five-coordinate opening. Across eight routed gate/up consumers a dense common input factor at full rank costs 1.25 times direct products; this is an exact preactivation-family arithmetic bound, not native timing or a whole-sum bound. The route comes from the installed GGUF, while the rank refers to the separately pinned original BF16 bank.

[The installed Q4_K gate/up co-route witness](installed-input-rank/README.md) proves full exact input rank **2,048** from the first 2,048 live decoded rows of five selected experts, although only 3,756 of 8,192 gate/up rows are nonzero on that layer-0 route. A dense exact shared linear input factor costs 1.25× the un-compacted direct preactivation products (1.545× the ideal exact-zero-row-compacted control); it cannot earn its cost by narrowing. This closes the original-BF16/installed-image mismatch for this restricted preactivation contract, not the composed nonlinear sum or native FP32 bits.

[The complete forty-layer two-expert gate/up census](installed-gateup-rank/README.md) certifies exact input rank 2,048 already from the first two selected Q4_K experts on 37/40 actual held token-1 routes. The other three have ranks 1,022/1,996/1,808, exactly their nonzero-row counts; no extra dependence appears among the 80,602 nonzero rows. Two experts are the minimum possible to expose 2,048 gate/up directions. This is a stronger all-layer preactivation obstruction to narrow **linear** shared inputs, not a whole-FFN, packed-code or native-speed lower bound.

[The decoded installed Q5_K co-route witnesses](installed-down-rank/README.md) prove **full 2,048 output rank already from four co-routed experts at layer 20**, so an exact dense eight-expert common output factor costs at least **1.5×** direct scalar products in that independent-hidden grammar. The contrasting layer-0 witness proves exact rank **2,006** on one held token's eight selected down experts: 2,090 of 4,096 decoded FP32 columns duplicate another column within their expert, yet a dense exact common-output factor still costs at least **1.469×** direct down MACs. The duplicates are dormant on that captured token; this is not a paid image or a new native gain. Its original-BF16 analogue has different coefficients.

[The exact original-BF16 rank witness](exact-route-rank/README.md) proves that four layer-0 down matrices already span all 2,048 output dimensions. A lossless shared linear basis for unrestricted expert hiddens therefore has no narrow rank on this bank: rank 2,048 costs 1.5 times direct routed-down MACs and 1.015625 times its equal-precision coefficient storage. [A co-routed held triple](output-factor-rank/README.md) independently certifies rank at least 1,530 on three original BF16 down maps whose IDs the installed GGUF selects together. Even a route-specific exact dense shared output factor costs at least 1.1206 times direct eight-expert down MACs on this triple. Producer-reachable quality and nonlinear codes are separate questions.

[Shared input subspaces on actual routes](input-subspace/README.md) project the layer-0 producer input through the complete eight-expert gate/up, SwiGLU and down weighted sum. ## Pinned map and native boundary

The official [Qwen3.6-35B-A3B config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B/raw/995ad96eacd98c81ed38be0c5b274b04031597b0/config.json) has 40 text layers, hidden width 2048, 256 routed experts, top eight per token, and intermediate width 512. It also has one separate shared expert; 30 layers use linear attention and ten use full attention. One routed expert has gate/up and down matrices totaling `3*2048*512 = 3,145,728` scalar weights. The shared expert, router, attention and embeddings are outside our proposed replacement, not free in model throughput.

The current llama.cpp CUDA/HIP implementation already groups assignments by expert in `ggml/src/ggml-cuda/mmid.cu` and `mmq.cuh`. In `mmq.cu` it quantizes broadcast gate/up inputs once per token and scatters those quantized activations to selected expert slots. Neither stable dispatch nor gate/up activation dedup is a discovery here. [`route_reuse.py`](route_reuse.py) remains a control for measuring occupancy and logical load budgets on native top-eight traces, not a proposal to replace that grouping.

The new observation is **after** the expert down projections. Let `h_e(x)` be each selected expert's already-computed nonlinear hidden vector, `W_e` its `[2048,512]` down matrix and `a_e` its router coefficient. The source real-valued response is

```
y = sum_selected a_e W_e h_e(x).
```

Choose a common orthonormal `C` of shape `[2048,r]`, and store expert factors `A_e = C.T W_e` of shape `[r,512]`. The proposed consumer computes

```
z = sum_selected a_e A_e h_e(x);   y_hat = C z.
```

The eight expert contributions now meet in `r` dimensions before **one** common projection back to 2048. This is not separate low-rank compression of eight down matrices: the output observer and router sum are explicitly composed. No original per-expert 2048-vector needs to be materialized. The native consumer would be grouped expert `A_e` matmuls, rank-slot scatter, a rank-order weighted reduction per token, then one shared `C` matmul. Gate/up quantization and routing remain as they are. If the final projection can fuse with a subsequent required output conversion, that must be measured; it is not assumed.

For arbitrary reachable hidden vectors, the map is exact in real arithmetic exactly when every selectable `W_e h_e` lies in `span(C)`. A sufficient condition for full hidden domains is `(I-CC.T)W_e=0` for every expert. The residual is `sum_e a_e (I-CC.T)W_e h_e`. Its norm is at most `sum_e |a_e| ||(I-CC.T)W_e||_F ||h_e||_2`. For isotropic zero-mean hidden inputs independently sampled by expert and a fixed distribution over selections and scores, minimizing expected squared error reduces to taking the leading eigenvectors of `sum_e P(e selected)*E[a_e²|e] W_e W_e.T`. The executable fits the equal-expert special case by eigendecomposition. This is a proof of the optimum for that **declared objective**, not for real routed activations or logits.

The construction changes the numerical map at `r<2048`. Even at full rank, a different FP32 dot-product or summation order need not reproduce native floating-point bits. Consumer quality requires routed activation traces and model loss. At the measured ranks the error already rejects this simple shared-output-basis family as a good default.

## Work and byte accounting

For one token, direct routed down work is `8*2048*512 = 8,388,608` multiply-accumulates. The composed map needs `8*r*512 + 2048*r = 6144r`; it wins on that arithmetic only for `r < 1365⅓`. This excludes unchanged gate/up (`8*2*2048*512` MACs), the shared expert (`3*2048*512`), router logits/top-eight selection, activation-dependent nonlinear work, factor quantization and memory movement. It also excludes the extra common-projection launch or fusion cost. There are eight `r`-length expert outputs to scatter and read if the kernel does not reduce them in place, `8*r*4` bytes for FP32 slot writes alone, plus reads and rank-order accumulation. The input-side 512 activations per expert still have to be read and the final 2048 output produced.

With equal effective bytes per coefficient `b`, direct down storage per layer is `256*2048*512*b`; factors plus common basis cost `(256*512*r + 2048*r)*b`. Their storage ratio is `r/2048 + r/(256*512)`, equal to .25390625 at rank 512. At illustrative `b=.5625` these are 150,994,944 versus 38,338,560 bytes per layer. These are *conditional representation budgets*: the CPU experiment used unquantized factors and float32 basis. A quantizer that preserves the measured approximation has not been built, and scale blocks, alignment, dynamic scale application, irregular expert groups and the common projection's cache behavior still require charge. The script prints those logical costs rather than claiming the GGUF image has these sizes.

A second control, `route_reuse.py`, takes real `{ "routes": [[eight IDs in original router rank], ...] }` JSON when the native baseline supplies it. It stable-sorts assignments, reconstructs original ranks, checks a deterministic FP32 reduction order, reports group occupancy and compares hypothetical token-major loads to finite expert-tile loads. Its byte margin includes an *upper* staging bill for input and partial-output scatter/gather plus assignment metadata, and its bandwidth number is an assumption. Since native MMQ already groups experts, **the token-major traffic figure is not the current baseline**. The native question is which tile size and group distribution the current kernel achieves, and whether a proposed new consumer beats that current implementation.

## Reproduce and next experiment

From the Kelana root with NumPy and SciPy:

```sh
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=8 OMP_NUM_THREADS=8 python3 research/moe/shared_down.py \
  /path/to/workspace/data/qwen-moe/experts/layer-0-0-16/down_proj.bf16 \
  --raw-experts 16 --train-experts 8 --ranks 128 256 512 --trials 8
python3 research/moe/route_reuse.py --batch 128 --family uniform --tile 16
python3 research/moe/route_reuse.py --trace /path/to/layer-routes.json --tile 16
```

The next decisive question is whether the **activation- and route-conditioned covariance** has a much smaller effective output rank than the weight-only isotropic panel. Collect held routed `h_e` and `a_e` for layer 0 under the native baseline, fit the covariance of `sum_e a_e W_e h_e` on separate calibration text, then evaluate held down outputs and end-to-end logits. An advantage would still need a quantized `A_e`/`C` representation and paired native grouped-matmul timing against existing `mul_mat_id`. If the held covariance remains broad, retire this family rather than hiding the .83 RMS loss behind a low MAC count.
