# A source-limited rotary score program and an obstruction on the captured source

**Question:** can the *complete score map* skip materializing Q and K as separately normalized 128-coordinate vectors? Yes for a declared low-dimensional source producer, but a matched source-span Q/K reader stores fewer bytes, and the existing complete captured source rules out the tempting low-rank extrapolation. This CPU-only study makes both boundaries explicit. It does not alter any model image, fit attention scores, time a GPU kernel, or claim the two-token construction serves Qwen's full vocabulary.

## Actual source and a 2D live score witness

The source is the pinned Qwen3-0.6B layer-0 fixture and checkpoint ([source-producer identity](../quip-token-producer/README.md)), token-file SHA256 `0479292cbee2cfc6cfc5f89e8f85dac85d2c0c275ed2704a59a7006faa7d66a2`, Q-input fixture SHA256 `389e5f8c17ac151070b12094849f450130ab9c1684f51b402d59fd4e7ac2008f`, BF16 checkpoint SHA256 `f47f71177f32bcd101b7573ec9171e6a57f4f4d31148d38e382306f42996874b` (source revision `c1899de289a04d12100db370d81485cdf75e47ca`). The fixture's first two distinct **held** token IDs, **17 and 16**, supply two actual captured 1,024-input producer vectors `u₀,u₁`; that deterministic first-distinct rule is not an error-based selection. The narrowly declared source interface supplies a live pair `α=(α₀,α₁)` representing `u=α₀u₀+α₁u₁`. Actual tokens 17/16 have one-hot α. Arbitrary mixtures are a mathematical continuation of the two observed states, **not** positions from the Qwen language source; a general 151,936-ID producer cannot return this two-dimensional label exactly.

Use the original **two 128-dimensional Q heads, their shared 128-dimensional GQA K head**, their unmodified Q/K RMSNorm gamma, epsilon `1e−6`, and RoPE pairs `(i,i+64)` with Qwen frequency `ωᵢ=1,000,000^(−i/64)`. Their 2×128 pre-norm projected basis arrays are prepared offline *from original BF16 weights*; they are **not** retained or read online by the fused endpoint. Three symmetric 2×2 Gram forms `G_Q0,G_Q1,G_K` determine the live norm factors `a_h(α)=1/sqrt((αᵀG_Qhα)/128+epsilon)` and `b(β)=1/sqrt((βᵀG_Kβ)/128+epsilon)`. For each causal relative distance `d=t−j∈[0,255]`, one 2×2 matrix `M_h,d` contracts the gamma-weighted basis pairs against `cos(dωᵢ)` and `sin(dωᵢ)`. The complete score is

```text
score_h(t,j) = a_h(α_t) b(α_j) · α_tᵀ M_h,t−j α_j .
```

The sign of the sine term follows `R_tᵀ R_j=R_(j−t)`, not an arbitrary pre-RoPE rotation. Both Q heads share the *same K norm and source label*; K is not silently specialized to one Q head. This formula preserves positive epsilon and diagonal gamma and never requests a Q or K vector online. The underlying unrounded law is an exact real-algebra factorization for **all α,β∈R² and every declared relative distance**, not merely a memorized table of the two tokens; the serialized FP64 coefficients realize it to the reported rounding tolerance. Its finite preparation is specified by [`build.py`](build.py); the stored [`score-image.bin`](score-image.bin) has SHA256 `6aff7977ca2569a77461d53079636fae7a280aba0337351b48697117c8d87454`.

| Paid model-specific score image | Bytes |
| --- | ---: |
| Two uint32 source-token IDs for the one-hot label router | 8 |
| Three symmetric 2×2 norm Gram forms, three FP64 fields each | 72 |
| Two Q heads ×256 relative distances ×2×2 FP64 score coefficients | 16,384 |
| **Total fused image** | **16,464** |

Relative distances beyond 255 need new coefficients or live trigonometric work; they are outside this finite format. The source embedding and its original layer-0 input RMSNorm remain a common upstream owner for both arms. For this declared two-ID runtime, the label router compares a live token ID to the two paid IDs and produces its pair of one-hot coefficients; mixed α requires a different authorized source producer. Any prepared Q/K basis and the offline 2×1,024 anchor states are preparation, not free inputs silently supplied to the inference reader. Original V head and O columns for the two Q heads remain **common unchanged** source fields (262,144+524,288 = **786,432 BF16 bytes**), and are actually used by [`replay.py`](replay.py) after score softmax. The peer Q head and shared K are included in both score and V/O continuation; nothing is dropped because it shares a key.

## Direct controls and completed causal endpoint

The strong full-source control is original BF16 `Q₀,Q₁,K` (three 128×1,024 matrices = **786,432 bytes**) plus shared Q/K RMSNorm gamma (512 B), or **786,944 bytes of Q/K state**, running three 128×1,024 projections and norms per position followed by two 128-term dot scores per visible query-key pair. This direct reader works beyond the two-token domain. The **matched conventional source-span Q/K control** stores all three actual gamma-weighted projected 128×2 linear maps (6,144 FP64 bytes), the same 72-byte pre-gamma norm Gram and 8-byte ID router: its committed [`span-qk-image.bin`](span-qk-image.bin), SHA256 `c166eaeb4b4ac9a36fc6f1927792c9ce37565b3a71ce7a19b38851500a21be3a`, is **6,224 B**. It does three two-coordinate projections and norm evaluations *per token*, retains Q0/Q1/shared K labels, then applies ordinary RoPE and 128-term scores; the gamma is already baked into the projected maps, while norm remains pre-gamma through the paid Gram. It is **10,240 B smaller** than the fused score image on this exact continuous span but does more per-score work. The original Q/K basis, gamma and anchor vectors used to prepare both images are not retained as additional runtime state.

A third, *stronger within the two-token discrete domain*, direct-score control stores the actual ideal score for each `(head,relative distance,query ID,key ID)`: its committed [`direct-token-scores.f64`](direct-token-scores.f64) is **16,384 bytes** plus the same 8-byte ID router, **16,392 B**. One addressed score load per pair beats the four-term 2×2 bilinear reader if only IDs 17 and 16 are admissible. The fused image spends **72 more bytes** to serve *every continuous α mixture*, for which that discrete table has no entry. Thus the fused factor trades larger static state than **either** source-limited control for fewer per-pair arithmetic terms than source-span Q/K, without an observed native runtime speedup. This is a storage/work tradeoff on a narrow source contract, not a general model-compression win.

The source slice's one-hot sequence repeats those actual two token IDs across **32 synthetic causal positions**. The direct full Q/K and independent fused-image readers compute all 1,056 visible scores of **both** heads, softmax causal histories, original shared V and both original O contributions. Greatest direct/fused score difference is **5.33e−15**, greatest combined post-O output difference **1.11e−16**, and combined post-O relative squared difference **2.26e−31**. The direct discrete score table agrees to **3.55e−15** per score and **5.55e−17** post-O. The matched **source-span Q/K** reader agrees to **3.55e−15** per score and **8.33e−17** post-O without storing original 1,024-input weights. A second 32-position sequence uses four alternating α values, including signed and mixed coefficients not representable by a token ID; fused and source-span Q/K readers both agree with direct full Q/K to at most **5.33e−15** per score and **1.11e−16** post-O, whereas the discrete table is inapplicable. These are FP64 computations on promoted BF16 stored weights, source states and gamma, with exact epsilon, FP64 RoPE/softmax/V/O. They are **not a byte-identical GPU/BF16 replay**: the existing BF16-rounding causal observer differs from the ideal direct scores by at most **.02210 / .02502** on the two heads in the discrete witness. The source sequence is composed of real token vectors but its alternating 32-token history is constructed here, not an observed Wikipedia window. No gold-token NLL or later-layer state equivalence follows.

The online arithmetic boundary is explicit. For a 32-position sequence the original full Q/K path performs approximately `3×128×1024=393,216` projection coefficient products per token before three norms, plus two 128-term score dots per visible pair. The matched source-span Q/K reader needs `3×128×2=768` label-products per token, three norms and the *same* two 128-term score dots, with RoPE. With α already supplied, fused score evaluation needs three 2×2 norm quadratics per token and **four bilinear coefficient products per head per visible pair** (plus two norm multiplications, relative-distance selection and a score-table read); both arms pay identical V/O work. The 2-token discrete direct table needs one score lookup per head and pair. No native lowering, cache timing, producer-label preparation timing or full-model throughput is claimed.

## Why the toy label cannot replace the source group

The actual train+held captured panel has 3,072 positions with **1,247 unique token IDs and exactly 1,247 distinct input states**. [`build.py`](build.py) evaluates the first 128 distinct token IDs' **BF16-rounded and gamma-weighted normalized** Q0, Q1 and shared K labels under the established [attention-consumer rounding contract](../attention-consumer/README.md). For each 128×128 dyadic float32 matrix it performs Gaussian elimination modulo the prime `2³¹−1`: the rank is **128/128 for all three** (witness ID lists and numerical singular values in [`build.json`](build.json)). A nonzero modular minor of dyadic rationals proves a nonzero rational minor. This applies to the represented BF16-rounded float32 labels **contracted in exact real arithmetic**, not to every emitted GPU/FP32 reduction order. The mode-zero score matrix over all `(query ID,key ID)` combinations therefore has rank 128 for either Q head, but **off-diagonal distinct IDs at relative position zero are not a reachable causal self-attention event**. That matrix alone is an extended raw-score-kernel certificate, not an attention-observer lower bound.

The causal bridge is a genuine **length-two history**. Put any selected token `y` at position 0, any selected query token `x` at position 1, and the query's self key `x` at position 1. Its probability odds are `log(p_prior/p_self)=score(q_x@1,k_y@0)−score(q_x@1,k_x@1)`. Comparing these odds for `y` and a fixed prior token `y₀` cancels both the self term and *any* query-dependent common score shift. `build.py` checks that the first 129 distinct source key labels augmented with a constant one have **modular rank 129** and both Q heads' first 128 labels after the **actual BF16-rounded position-one RoPE** have rank **128**. Therefore the matrix of centered prior-key odds across those 128 queries and 128 alternative prior tokens has **exact rank 128** over the rational dyadic score factors (the common `1/sqrt(128)` scalar does not affect rank). This rules out an exact `<128`-channel separable key-label bilinear odds map with query labels depending only on the current token and key labels depending only on the prior token, even after row-offset freedom, on those *reachable two-token histories*. A candidate whose query label itself incorporates prior history is outside this factor class. It is an exact rank threshold, **not** an approximate rank-error floor or a rejection of a paid nonseparable lookup, nonlinear program, different source representation or longer causal behavior.

A direct FP64 `(Q head,relative distance,query ID,key ID)` table for just the **1,247 observed unique IDs** would cost `2×256×1247²×8 = 6,369,316,864 bytes` before ID routing. At all **151,936** vocabulary IDs it is **94,554,309,001,216 bytes** (~94.6 TB), before handling unseen histories or context-dependent source changes. This sharply contrasts the two-ID table's 16,384 bytes. The exact two-dimensional construction remains useful only if an actual producer supplies such a label on its reachable domain (or a richer source program achieves a different compression); simply calling every observed token a short label does not make its full score table small. The existing [transported-norm](../transported-norm/README.md) and [complete-head observer](../quip-complete-head-observer/README.md) show why normalization/gamma and full causal V/O cannot be omitted when changing the coordinate boundary; this study keeps all of them.
