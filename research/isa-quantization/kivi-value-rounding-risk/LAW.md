# Frozen adjacent-grid source law (declared before scores)

Original K2/V2 after-query V33 flush and FP16 `(low, step)` fields, K records, recent BF16 bytes and all query timing stay unchanged. The scalar source is the *original BF16 V coordinate*, converted exactly to FP64 for probability arithmetic; field construction remains from the original source, never from a feedback target. Each of the four decoder levels is evaluated as `float32(low + float32(step * j))` with distinct FP32 multiplication and addition (no FMA), `j=0..3`. For `v` strictly between consecutive *distinct* ordered decoder levels `a<=v<=b`, draw the lower level with probability `(b-v)/(b-a)` and upper with `(v-a)/(b-a)`, evaluated in FP64; at a level, choose its smallest-index digit deterministically. Below/above the representable endpoint range, choose the closest endpoint deterministically. Duplicate levels and zero step use the smallest-index matching digit, yielding zero variance; all draws are independent over KV-head/token/coordinate/flush, with one draw shared by both associated query heads. There is no FP32 RNG implementation claim. All original deterministic codes and records are only the reference, not alternate fields for this law.

Readout: original K2 attention probabilities (the existing FP32 CPU softmax contract) are fixed; FP64 value mixing and FP64 complete original-O linear projection determine mean and deterministic comparators. Expected SSE is squared FP64 mean-to-teacher difference plus the FP64 covariance trace. No stochastic samples, tuning, stochastic K, changed recent values or changed teacher. The FP32 reference CPU receipt remains a separate rounding boundary; it is not equated to this FP64 readout. The four-level source encoder would additionally require bracket finding (up to three level comparisons per coordinate), FP64 probability/subtraction/division (or equivalent), a uniform random draw and selection at each V flush; no measured hardware cost or paid win follows from an ideal-law screen.
