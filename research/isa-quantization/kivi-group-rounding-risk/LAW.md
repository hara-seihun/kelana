# Canonical within-G32 systematic adjacent rounding law

At each original V flush, for each of its four original coordinate-order G32 groups, read the same adjacent *actual decoded FP32* levels `l_j,h_j` and clipped upper probability `f_j` as the pinned independent owner [0bed18b2](../kivi-value-rounding-risk/LAW.md). Duplicate or exact endpoint levels have `f_j=0`. Let `S_0=0`, `S_{j+1}=S_j+f_j` in ideal real arithmetic, with `j=0..31` in the original group order. Draw one `U~Uniform[0,1)` per original group/token/KV head, independently across groups and flushes. Set `Z_j=floor(S_{j+1}+U)-floor(S_j+U)` and store the lower/upper adjacent digit according to `Z_j`. A digit is shared by both Q heads of its KV head. No future token/source is needed: all 32 arriving coordinates already reside in this original V record. No coordinate permutation, fitted phase, persistent moment, new metadata field or precision change is part of this law.

For each `j`, `Z_j` is Bernoulli(`f_j`). Its event is a circular interval of length `f_j`, starting at `(-S_{j+1}) mod 1`; a zero-length interval contributes no event. The group count telescopes to `floor(S_32+U)-floor(U)`, whose distance from `S_32` is strictly less than one (and equals `S_32` when that is an integer). The actual output increment is `w_j Z_j`, where `w_j=h_j-l_j` can vary by coordinate after field rounding. Thus a balanced *count* is not a balanced weighted output.

For intervals `I_j` on the circle, `Cov(Z_j,Z_k)=length(I_j intersect I_k)-f_j f_k`. The intersection is computed by splitting each wrapping interval into its two pieces on `[0,1)`. A separate bounded-record witness enumerates consecutive intervals between all circular breakpoints, chooses a midpoint only for evaluation, and contracts their exact interval lengths with the resulting projected O support vectors. This is deterministic integration, **not RNG sampling**. With weights `a_r>=0` and centered outcomes `z_r-f`, the covariance equals `sum_r a_r(z_r-f)(z_r-f)^T`, so it is PSD without relying on floating-point eigenvalue tests. FP64 numerical accumulation represents the ideal-real law; documented tolerances check its implementation.

The owner law's complete-O mean output is unchanged. For an aged V token `i` of KV head `h`, compute three shared-head covariance contractions over its four independently drawn groups: `c00_i=tr(O0 Cov(V_i) O0^T)`, `c01_i=tr(O0 Cov(V_i) O1^T)`, `c11_i=tr(O1 Cov(V_i) O1^T)`. The fixed K2 attention probabilities give per-query `Var_O=sum_i (p0_i² c00_i+2p0_i p1_i c01_i+p1_i² c11_i)`. Add this to the *unchanged pinned mean-bias SSE* for expected complete-output SSE. Original K2/recent BF16/O/teacher, fields, and flush chronology remain fixed. The sampled runtime of this ideal law and implementation cost of a random source are not established by these exact-risk scores.
