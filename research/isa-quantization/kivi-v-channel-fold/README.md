# Folded V-channel grouping: a same-byte KIVI cache coordinate control

**Result.** On the original Qwen3-0.6B layer-0 two-query-head/one-KV-head causal source, one train-only V-channel grouping changes KIVI G32/R32 4-bit held GQA-pair complete-O relative squared error from **0.00015535488 to 0.00014332225 (7.75% lower)**, without increasing either its **52,400 B peak / 46,592 B final causal cache** or the original **1,573,376 B static Q/K/V/O/gamma source group**. Train changes 0.00013661175 → 0.00012607632 (7.71% lower). This is a known *channel-rearrangement technique* applied to a fixed local control, not an invented quantizer, an upstream KIVI configuration, a full-model result, a latency measurement or a SOTA claim. The stronger but larger [V45 recent-buffer control](../kivi-v45-control/README.md) and [query-metric control](../kivi-response-metric/README.md) recover respectively 11.88% and 13.0% held error at +2,288 and +2,304 bytes, so same-size coordinate folding is a distinct tradeoff rather than their replacement.

## Fixed rule, chronology and source provenance

Before candidate scoring, we declared a *single* stable ascending permutation of the 128 value channels by RMS of the **BF16-rounded V projection** on all 2,048 training source rows; ties use original channel index. Four consecutive 32-channel sets then become KIVI's existing value-token min/max groups. The train RMS ranges in sorted groups are [.108718,.115481], [.115502,.119454], [.119466,.123145], [.124286,.295296]; original group maxima are .23951,.13449,.29530,.14254. This is grouping similar source scales, not reassigning individual codes, changing the min/max quantizer, choosing a grouping from held output, or ensuring all outliers vanish. The [value-coordinate transport study](../value-coordinate-gauge/README.md) states the exact source gauge, grouped-quantizer partition quotient and complete fixed-probability multihead Gram; source RMS itself is not that output objective. The exact new-to-old indices and per-original-channel train RMS are in [`source-image.json`](source-image.json). Source fixture SHA256 `389e5f8c17ac151070b12094849f450130ab9c1684f51b402d59fd4e7ac2008f`; BF16 checkpoint revision `c1899de289a04d12100db370d81485cdf75e47ca`, SHA256 `f47f71177f32bcd101b7573ec9171e6a57f4f4d31148d38e382306f42996874b` (same as [original KIVI](../kivi-causal-cache/README.md)). The checkpoint has no V/O bias in this layer. The rule, full byte bill, and train0 gate were sent to the coordinator before computing the candidate.

For permutation `p[new]=old`, [`prepare.py`](prepare.py) writes the actual **128×1024 BF16 V rows** `V'[new,:]=V[p[new],:]` ([`v-replacement.bf16`](v-replacement.bf16), 262,144 B, SHA256 `54b649480a54f6ee8f02e141590747a795af7b090beed8a555544cf06bd01f23`) and **1024×256 BF16 O columns** `O'_h[:,new]=O_h[:,p[new]]` in **both** Q heads ([`o-replacement.bf16`](o-replacement.bf16), 524,288 B, SHA256 `ec728d7131f96389d2dac5124afe3a0a520a4579a7d65d740c54d4e67d0ae28e`). They replace exactly **786,432 B** of original static weights, *not* an additional 786,432 B. The other GQA groups' V and O slices remain in their original positions. `O'_h V'=O_h V` as linear maps (accounting for row/column orientation), while actual BF16 V projection and the final FP32 O sum incur only reordered floating-point reduction. One shared V permutation suffices for both Q heads; separate head permutations would violate shared GQA state. No permutation table, online gather/scatter, new gamma/scale, or runtime feature table is needed when these physical replacement weights are installed together. The retained JSON permutation and training RMS are **offline provenance**, not inference metadata; static application tooling would replace these two source slices together.

Primary prior art: Duanmu et al., [SKVQ, *Sliding-window Key and Value Cache Quantization for Large Language Models* (COLM 2024), §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06219v3#S3.SS1) and its [official implementation](https://github.com/cat538/SKVQ), rearrange KV channels so groups have more similar distributions and fuse the rearrangement into model projections. SKVQ also uses its own calibration/reordering, clipped dynamic quantization and sliding-window settings: **our stable train-RMS sort is a local adaptation, not SKVQ's official ordering or a SKVQ reproduction**. [KIVI](../kivi-causal-cache/README.md) supplies the actual asymmetric causal group quantizer and its source-specific BF16/FP16 adaptation here. We do not attribute folded source grouping to upstream KIVI or claim either paper's full method/results.

Before opening any held candidate result, the admission gate was: (a) independent BF16 image-reader/source equality, original K flush events byte-identical, same actual cache sizes, and original source complete-O within `1e−4` maximum absolute coordinate; (b) packed train-window-0 pair-O relative SSE **strictly smaller** than original 0.000145640715. The gate passed: unchanged eight K events, source max difference `1.34e−7`, candidate `0.000133205277`. [`gate.json`](gate.json) records the fixed decision. No permutation, scale, seed, group or bit rate was changed afterward. All 8 training and 4 inspected-held windows use exactly this frozen source image.

## Independent paid replay and observed results

[`source_image.py`](source_image.py) checks SHA256 and independently decodes both physical BF16 weight images. [`source.py`](source.py) produces V from the **decoded V weight rows**, loads the **decoded O column bytes**, and carries original Q, K, RoPE/gamma and unpermuted teacher; [`check.py`](check.py) independently checks image words against original BF16 checkpoint slices, V BF16 source words, uncompressed original-vs-permuted complete-O, and all 8 causal K flush records against the frozen original for every window. [`run.py`](run.py) invokes the original pinned KIVI [`build.py`](../kivi-causal-cache/build.py) and **separately** [`replay.py`](../kivi-causal-cache/replay.py) with the changed physical source: the reader does not import the builder's group quantization/packing functions. It independently decodes each V nibble and FP16 origin/step field, verifies it against the currently available BF16 source values, and reads each emitted causal flush only after its query. All original 256 before/after prefix hashes, selected snapshots, final image, event log and exact byte checks are preserved in the local `*-manifest.json`, `*-{pre,post,final,flush}.bin`, `*-result.json` receipts. The flush log is evidence, **not resident cache state**. [`results.json`](results.json) asserts all 12 original-vs-folded K-event equalities, both source/image hashes and byte ledgers, and aggregates raw SSE/reference-square sums before division. Uncompressed original-versus-folded complete-O maximum absolute coordinate across windows is `7.31e−7` train / `7.26e−7` held; attention KL matches original because Q/K and scores are unchanged.

The cache ledger is identical to the original KIVI: eight 2,560-B K slabs (nibble codes plus per-channel FP16 origin/step), 224 final 80-B V tokens (nibbles plus four FP16 origin/step pairs), 32 recent BF16 V rows, and causal preflush K/V recent high-water. The current CPU reader still expands the paid packed cache to up to 262,144 B of **transient FP32 K/V scratch per query** plus observer arrays; it is **not** a native 52 KB complete process or a claimed speedup. Offline train preparation computes 2,048×128 projected BF16 V outputs, 128 RMS reductions, a stable 128-key sort, then physically writes/reorders 786,432 B of weights. Online projected V and both complete O matrix-product work shapes are unchanged, and K key preparation/flush and softmax work are unchanged. Token-local V min/max/code work (four groups of 32) and decode remain, with changed grouping. No GPU, new capture, candidate sweep or future-token grouping occurs.
